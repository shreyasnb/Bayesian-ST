# Claims, computations and evidence

The unified study uses passive proper-complex snapshots, `u=h z+e`, with covariance `s I+q P`, `P=h h^H`, and complex rank one. The filter receives `u`; the desired steering vector enters a later beamforming decision. Latent coefficients and true directions enter generation and scoring only. This is a different likelihood from the earlier real, known-input contact example.

## Why scalar inclusion can fail under a correct stationary model

A central scalar credible interval is a different object from a state region. Suppose the fixed query equals the true line, `v=h_true`, and powers are known. Then the true functional is `v^H P_true v=1`, the upper boundary of its support. For every finite, nondegenerate continuous posterior, its 97.5% quantile is strictly below one. The central 95% interval therefore excludes that truth with probability one, even in the correctly specified stationary experiment. Orthogonal queries analogously attain the lower support boundary. For scanning covariance power, the endpoints are `s` and `s+q`.

This is neither a failure of the state-coverage proof nor evidence of inaccurate quantiles. It explains why a fixed ULA line and an exactly aligned scan query can have poor central scalar inclusion. The proof's equivariance applies to state inclusion; it does not make arbitrary fixed-query central intervals uniformly valid confidence intervals. Posterior scalar probability, fixed-state frequentist inclusion, and prior-averaged Bayesian calibration must be distinguished. A common state-region envelope does inherit the state-inclusion event, giving at least that fixed-time enclosure probability under the ideal stationary assumptions above. Outside those assumptions it is a posterior image, with its actual empirical performance reported separately.

## What a comparison can support

The framework supplies a distribution for decisions and diagnostics: a conditional expected-power action, directional posterior risk, state regions and functional uncertainty, and a coherent hierarchy when powers are unknown. Those outputs carry additional computational cost. The modal estimator alone duplicates weighted covariance PCA under matching assumptions. A cheap geometric tracker can win on runtime and can also win on actual loss in particular regimes.

A persuasive empirical advantage must survive the one-snapshot equality check, matched isotropization, continuously validated scalar regularization, broad covariance loading and geometric tracking, with independent trajectory-level comparisons. Report the complete SNR/snapshot map, including losses and numerical failures. The theory guarantees a conditional model-based decision identity; it does not predetermine those empirical rankings. The physical moving ULA is a deliberate mismatch test for the invariant stochastic process and has no ground-truth `kappa`.

## Observed paper evidence

The complete fixed-power decision map uses 256 independent test and 128 validation trajectories per generation-law/noise condition, at seven horizons. Across its 42 cells, paired pointwise 95% intervals favor the mean over the mode in 42 cells and GROUSE in 42 cells. Against matched isotropization there are five gains, seven losses, 24 intervals crossing zero and six exact one-snapshot equalities. Against validated regularized PCA there are ten gains, 28 losses and four crossing intervals; loading gives 13 gains, 26 losses and three crossing intervals. These are exploratory cell counts, not a multiple-comparison-adjusted discovery claim.

The regularized-PCA comparison separates by generation law: ten gains and no losses in the 14 matched-Haar cells, but losses in all 28 physical-ULA cells. The static ULA likelihood remains correct; its fixed steering geometry and decision-risk weighting differ from Haar-prior averaging. This does not contradict stationary state-inclusion invariance. The isotropized posterior retains data-dependent spread, while the regularized-PCA scalar is shared across test trajectories within each validation condition/horizon. The largest observed positive point estimate of the relative power gain from retaining anisotropy is only 0.1515%, with mixed signs across cells. The simulations support the value of adaptive uncertainty under the matched prior, not a substantial universal anisotropy or runtime advantage.

All 12 ordinary native mean-feasibility targets and all 1,152 evaluated native process-tail reports resolve within their declared numerical budgets; the two deliberate series-cap failures remain. Four of 192 CP1 full-reference reports retain unresolved status. Small inverse-fit residuals do not eliminate dynamic density approximation: the Bingham-versus-reference total variation ranges from 0.00166 to 0.145, while the largest inverse-fit residual is about `5e-9`. The CP1 result is not an exact eight-channel reference, and unresolved reports are retained rather than used as verified comparators.

The modal certificate is valid in 256 of 4,864 fixed-time reports, all from the long stationary control, with no observed valid-bound violations. Its failure at practical short/drifting horizons is a failure of a conservative sufficient condition, not a theorem violation. Across eight stationary state/time/region entries, observed inclusion ranges from 0.914 to 0.977. The lowest is HPD inclusion at the ULA 20-degree state and N=8: 234/256, with pointwise Wilson interval [0.873, 0.943]. This deviation is retained; the finite experiment is not blanket confirmation of exact 95% state coverage.

The physical drift limitation is stronger: at `s=.2`, both state regions miss in all four predeclared memory/time cells, with 0/32 inclusion in each, despite all 128 associated numerical reports resolving. The selected memory optimizes validation decision power, not state coverage. Nominal posterior mass therefore does not provide calibrated state uncertainty under this unmodeled drift. This is separate from the central-scalar support-boundary limitation explained above.

All 832 joint inference fits complete without calculation failures. In the stationary cohort, all 768 state-mean and covariance-mean checks pass, 618 parameter checks pass, and 167 fits meet all finite-chain diagnostic flags. All 64 moving fits retain unresolved concentration and covariance-mean diagnostics. These budgets support implemented conditionals and some stable stationary decision means; they do not establish calibrated full moving-state or concentration inference. Native n=8, kappa=20 process prediction through T=24 has median cumulative wall time 1.44 s, mean 2.02 s and maximum 21.2 s. These shared-host measurements are not dedicated latency or a complexity law and exclude other pipeline stages.
