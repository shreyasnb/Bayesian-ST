# Proposed figure captions

These notes are external to the title-free PDFs. Page and panel conditions are in figure_manifest.json.

## setup.pdf

Available measurements and task geometry. Beamforming uses interference-only proper-complex snapshots; the desired unit steering vector enters the distortionless decision. The displayed beampattern uses a predeclared saved simulation, not another independent repetition. Contact uses an independently solved maintained bilateral constraint with known resistance, applied input and directly measured noisy velocity. Positions and normals are generation/scoring information.


## task_performance.pdf

Beamforming: $\ell_t=10\log_{10}[(w_t^{\mathrm H}R_tw_t)/p_t^\star]$, where $p_t^\star=(a^{\mathrm H}R_t^{-1}a)^{-1}$. The stationary panel uses exact saved covariance output at $t=n\in\{32,64,128\}$; curves average 256 Monte Carlo simulations and shading gives the posterior-mean pointwise Student 95\% interval. Settings remain those selected using additional noisy measurements. Supplementary moving-array boxes use exact covariance output-power differences between methods on the same simulation; boxes are distributions rather than confidence intervals. Contact: $\mathcal E_t(D_t)=\frac13\sum_{j=1}^3\|D_tv_t^{(j)}-P_{\mathcal X_t}v_t^{(j)}\|^2$, $v_t^{(j)}=0.02R_t^\top e_j$ m/s. Saved three-input errors are multiplied by $10^6$ to obtain $(\mathrm{mm}/\mathrm{s})^2$, with no additional division. Panel (a) uses the stationary inputs at $t=1,4,16,32$ and the posterior-mean confidence band. Panel (b) first averages over $t=4,8,\ldots,192$ in each of 256 sphere simulations, then computes each method mean and pointwise Student 95\% interval. The supplementary paired panel uses absolute error differences from these same simulations. GREAT uses a complex Grassmann update for the array and receives measured velocities and frame rotations for contact; conditional contact methods also receive the known forces. This cohort has 256 independent test episodes per condition; snapshots and queries are averaged within each episode.


## posterior_anisotropy.pdf

Contact: the saved concentrated-input posterior at $t=16$ has normal $e_3$. Panels (a,b) show $q_{-A_t^+}([n])$ and its matched isotropic law with the same mode and expected squared chordal distance. The fixed-radius spheres retain their native coordinates, common camera and logarithmic density scale. Colors are density relative to normalized invariant measure; antipodal normals represent one plane. The point marks their common modal line. Panel (c) plots $r_t(v(\theta))/v_0^2$, with $v(\theta)=v_0(\cos\theta\,e_2+\sin\theta\,e_3)$ and $v_0=0.02$ m/s. Each curve uses its own mean in $r_t(v)=v^\top[\overline P_t^+-(\overline P_t^+)^2]v$; the saved unit-input curve already equals this normalized quantity. The supplementary constructed example separates equal centered chordal-distance laws from directional response variance. Beamforming shows a real-coefficient section $h=U_3x$, $x\in S^2$, of the complex-line density relative to its mode, rather than a normalized marginal. Its supplementary empirical CDF uses exact saved covariance output-power reductions at $n=t=64$. The actual posterior is the fixed concentrated-input simulation 0 at t=16. Directional density and repeated-simulation task performance are separate comparisons. Spheres retain the original saved physical/coefficient coordinates and shared oblique camera. The blue-purple-pink-yellow palette uses the unchanged shared logarithmic density scale; its four-decade display floor does not mean zero density. Color positions are not probability quantiles. Thin gray curves are front-visible coordinate great circles, not density contours or credible regions. No mode-facing rotation, density deformation or additional posterior fitting is used. In the directional-risk panel, theta is in radians and the indicated mean is either the full posterior mean or the manuscript-defined isotropic mean; each curve is its own belief risk.

## information.pdf

Directional likelihood information for the original concentrated-input contact fixture at $t=16$. The generating normal is $n_0=e_3$, $X_0=(e_1,e_2)$, and the plane graph chart has $z=(z_1,z_2)^\top$ and normal $n(z)=(n_0-X_0z)/\sqrt{1+\|z\|^2}$. For the same frozen prescribed inputs and true variance, $p_z$ is the joint Gaussian response law with means $P_{\mathcal X(z)}v_j$ and covariance $\sigma^2I_3$. Panel (a) shows equal values of the exact divergence $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_0\|p_z)=z^T\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cond}}z/[2(1+\|z\|^2)]$ in native chart coordinates, with equal axis scales. These are likelihood-divergence contours, not credible or confidence regions. Panel (b) evaluates the same divergence along the two native information directions; $\delta$ is the physical angle between $n_0$ and $n(z)$. The marked rotations $5^\circ$ and $30^\circ$ produce nearly equal divergences, approximately $0.2396$ and $0.2308$, despite their different angular sizes. Thus this input design distinguishes rotations much more strongly in one direction. The local limit is $D_{\mathrm{KL}}=\tfrac12z^T\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cond}}z+o(\|z\|^2)$. This is expected observation-law information at the generating plane, distinct from finite-data posterior curvature. No new observations, inference, fitting, or experimental repetitions are introduced.


## posterior_curvature.pdf

Invariant-density Hessian and local accuracy diagnostics. Empirical bounded log-coordinate spread is compared with $H_A^{-1}$ only under recorded locality conditions; broad/nonlocal cases remain visible. The stationary inverse-Fisher study uses fixed known variances and $\beta=1$, reports estimation bias and local fractions, and treats inverse Fisher as a local reference rather than a global intrinsic Cramer--Rao equality. The real graph-density volume term is a separate quantity, retained in the tables. Forgetting sampling references use squared weights.


## tracking_bound.pdf

Recomputed sharp fixed-time envelope $\sqrt{\min(k,n-k)}\,\epsilon_t/(\gamma_t-\epsilon_t)$ under $\epsilon_t<\gamma_t/2$, with the exact aggregate conditional drift and the appropriate real/complex constants. True path, gap and drift are analysis quantities, not online confidence radii. Invalid conditions remain marked and receive no violation test. Error bands describe across-simulation spread. The geometric ceiling is not used to clip the theorem. Phase tables and exact binomial violation intervals retain every declared condition.


## block_updates.pdf

Exact discounted aggregation, delayed actions and constant-subspace approximation are separate checks. Aggregation retains the boundary factor $\beta^L$, within-block weights, per-observation variances and recorded noncommuting transport. Numerical zeros are displayed at a $10^{-16}$ plotting floor; full tolerances remain in the tables. Delayed actions are evaluated only after a block arrives, with common endpoints and physical output age reported separately. The block-likelihood reference resets to the uniform prior, $A_0=0$, and uses $q_{\sum_{j=t-L+1}^t B_j}$ with $\beta=1$ within the raw $L$-observation window. It does not retain the singleton history. Under motion its one-state likelihood is an approximation, distinct from the algebraic aggregation identity. Processing and task-decision cost is reported in the computation output.


## additional_comparisons.pdf

Complete declared dimension, memory and method comparisons. Heatmaps show comparator-minus-full posterior-mean loss; positive values favor the full mean. Hatching marks cells whose paired pointwise Student 95\% interval includes zero. No simultaneous-coverage claim is made. Test episodes never tune a setting. Paired differences include losses and inconclusive intervals; broad grids are exploratory without simultaneous coverage. Contact GREAT is response-only and cannot isolate the benefit of known inputs. The same-parameter local Gaussian and recursive EKF are distinct supplementary references with their validity diagnostics retained. Identical modal/PCA or conditional spectral least-squares solutions are not counted as independent algorithms.


## variance_estimation.pdf

Fixed-boundary stationary-block EM reference. Every E-step reconstructs the full block from one saved boundary prior. Evidence curves are shifted by their own initial value, so final heights do not rank starts; absolute normalized evidence selects starts. Both positive starts and the uniform-prior zero-signal boundary are retained. Noise floors and pooled isotropic M-steps are tested in the numerical tables. Contact residual updates include posterior response uncertainty, not only a squared residual using the mean projector. These reference blocks are not online adaptive-tracker simulations.


## predictive_calibration.pdf

Actual-recursion calibration with distinct state, clean-response and noisy-next-observation targets. Pages separate joint prior-predictive sampling, fixed-state repetitions and drift diagnostics. Vertical intervals are pointwise exact binomial uncertainty across independent Monte Carlo simulations, not credible uncertainty. All intervals are evaluated at their declared time before the scored future observation is seen. Appended pages show 95% report-size distributions at t=64 for every probability regime. State-ball sizes are chordal radii, obtained by taking the square root of each recorded squared-distance upper threshold; HPD energy thresholds are not used as geometric widths. Scalar sizes are upper minus lower in the n=3 fixture units. Boxes show the median and interquartile range across independent Monte Carlo simulations, with 1.5-IQR whiskers and every outlier; this spread is not a posterior distribution or a confidence interval over width. Proper-score checks are separate. Validation-fitted widths do not establish a matched-coverage advantage if achieved test coverage misses its target. Numerical mass and quantile precision are separate from mean accuracy. Both applications use n=3 reliability fixtures. On the inclusion pages, panel (a) checks latent-subspace inclusion in its HPD set; panel (b) reports the clean scalar $e_1^T Pq$ for a known unit real query $q$; panel (c) reports the noisy scalar $\operatorname{Re}(u_{t,1})$. Contact observations are real. On the appended size pages, panels (a), (b), (c) show the state-ball chordal radius, clean scalar width and noisy scalar width, respectively. These are 128 independent test episodes per fixed checkpoint and regime.

## model_mismatch.pdf

Declared mismatch experiments under the actual recursion. Beamforming changes training contamination, source powers/geometry, sensor covariance or motion speed. Contact changes constrained mechanics or sensor construction, including dissipative friction/sticking and contact loss. Known anisotropic resistance requires transformed measurements and the correct sensor covariance; isotropic physical velocity noise becomes $\sigma^2D$. Position differencing induces correlated velocity errors. Increased posterior spread is not a repair of an incorrect likelihood.


## computation_time.pdf

Panel (a) includes all 64 parameter corrections and task decisions every one or sixteen observations; its displayed curves exclude optional uncertainty reports. Panel (b) separates parameter updates, task decisions, uncertainty reports, EM and other time for the same 64-observation workload. Row labels state task-decision intervals; optional uncertainty reports and EM fits occur every 32 observations. EM rows use two or four iterations. Phase stacks sum per-phase medians and need not equal median total elapsed time. The separate memory page distinguishes stored arrays from tracked allocations and excludes unmeasured native workspaces/RSS. Repeated clocks measure execution variability on a fixed statistical state, not independent task-performance simulations or worst-case deadlines.
