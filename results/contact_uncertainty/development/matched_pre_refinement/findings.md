# Completed matched run before the numerical-backend refinement

This is the completed, frozen 400-trajectory run, retained as development history. It is superseded by a fresh execution using the refined inverse-moment backend. The physical-validation numerical failure that motivated that refinement was independent of these matched outcomes. No matched inputs, process parameters, seeds, reporting times, reference subsets, integration budgets or acceptance thresholds were selected from this run.

Both concentrations use 200 independent trajectories. The first sixteen trajectory identifiers at each concentration receive the full-density reference. Terminal comparisons average the original nine query directions within each trajectory; queries and components are not independent replications. `paired_effects.csv` reports exploratory paired 95% Student t intervals without dropping local-approximation or reference failures.

At kappa = 4, the recursive Gaussian has clean vector MSE 0.21124 versus 0.13291 for the process Bingham method. The paired difference is 0.07834 [0.06107, 0.09561]. Its clean interval score is 1.27053 versus 0.81886, a difference of 0.45167 [0.33799, 0.56534]. At kappa = 20 these differences are smaller: 0.009934 [0.002353, 0.017515] for MSE and 0.09854 [0.01725, 0.17983] for interval score. These comparisons assess the whole recursive approximation, including nonlinear measurement updates and process propagation.

The same-parameter Laplace control does not show a resolved MSE or interval-score benefit for retaining the global Bingham shape: both paired intervals include zero at both concentrations. Its terminal local-approximation flag fails for 26/200 targets at kappa = 4 and 1/200 at kappa = 20; all are retained. These flags concern non-negligible mass beyond the local projective chart, rather than failed mapped-Gaussian numerical integration. This control prevents attributing the recursive-Gaussian difference entirely to the global distribution family.

Two of 96 predeclared reference reports fail their numerical acceptance checks. At kappa = 4, trial 0, step 16, interval-endpoint refinement is 0.02107 of the fine interval width, exceeding the 0.02 limit. At kappa = 20, trial 10, step 24, coarse degree-24 negative spectral mass is 1.00623e-9, just above the 1e-9 limit; the degree-32 prediction remains positive. The latter was diagnosed at the unchanged budgets in `fixed_budget_spectral_diagnostic.json`. Both failures remain in all tables; there is no success-selected reference subset and no unconditional claim that reference tails are resolved.

Small inverse residuals are separate from distribution accuracy. Across the fixed reference subset, average process-Bingham/reference density TV differences are about 0.0227 and 0.0478 at kappa = 4 and 20 respectively. The corresponding average mean-operator errors are 0.0080 and 0.0160. Thus accurate one-step moment matching does not make the recursively projected density exact.

This is a dimensionless matched-process control, not the physical robot-contact experiment or a fixed-state coverage theorem. Known process concentrations, a six-observation data-only Gaussian warm-up, and clean response intervals are used. The fixed-forgetting method is a diagnostic rather than an independently tuned physical benchmark. Measured wall times come from a shared, concurrently loaded execution environment and are not hard real-time guarantees.
