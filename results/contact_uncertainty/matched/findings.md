# Matched process comparison

This control uses a known Watson transition on real Gr(2,3), a uniform initial normal line, coordinate inputs, T = 24 and sensor variance 0.12. There are 200 independent trajectories at each of kappa = 4 and 20. The first sixteen trajectory identifiers at each concentration receive the independent full-density reference, at the unchanged development-selected resolutions. This is a dimensionless process experiment, not the mechanical contact plant or a fixed-state coverage theorem.

The final execution accepted the 135-test numerical gate before opening its observations. All 400 scientific checkpoint results, excluding timing fields, are exactly equal to the completed pre-refinement run archived under `../development/matched_pre_refinement/`. The inverse-moment refinement changes numerical handling of other, independently observed physical-validation targets; it does not change this experiment's results. `refinement_equivalence.json` records that comparison.

## What the controlled comparison shows

At kappa = 4, terminal clean vector MSE is 0.13291 for process Bingham and 0.21124 for the recursive Gaussian. The paired Gaussian-minus-Bingham difference is 0.07834 [0.06107, 0.09561]. Clean interval score is 0.81886 versus 1.27053, a difference of 0.45167 [0.33799, 0.56534]. At kappa = 20, the corresponding MSE difference is 0.009934 [0.002353, 0.017515], and the interval-score difference is 0.09854 [0.01725, 0.17983]. These are exploratory, two-sided 95% paired Student t intervals across 200 trajectories. The original nine query directions and their components are averaged within a trajectory; they are not independent replications.

The process Bingham clean-component coverage is 0.9550 and 0.9528, compared with 0.8600 and 0.9198 for the recursive Gaussian. Its intervals are wider: 0.7653 and 0.5815 versus 0.7143 and 0.5382. The proper interval-score comparison is therefore more informative than coverage alone. All process-Bingham and recursive-Gaussian report calculations resolve at their declared budgets.

These differences concern the **whole recursive approximation**. The local Gaussian matches the Watson kernel's angular variance, rather than its entire transition law or its exact projector mean. It also linearizes the nonlinear measurement update. The Gaussian is initialized from the first six process-Bingham corrections; both methods use those observations and the initialization time is counted. The comparison does not identify a benefit from global posterior shape alone.

The same-parameter Laplace control addresses that separate question: both MSE and interval-score paired intervals include zero at both concentrations. Its terminal MSE differences are 0.000450 [-0.002523, 0.003422] and -0.000273 [-0.000915, 0.000369]. Interval-score differences are 0.00738 [-0.02337, 0.03812] and -0.00215 [-0.02336, 0.01906]. This experiment does not establish a material average predictive benefit from evaluating the full Bingham distribution instead of the same-parameter local approximation. The latter's local-approximation flag fails for 26/200 terminal targets at kappa = 4 and 1/200 at kappa = 20; all remain in the paired results. Across all reporting times there are 84 local-approximation failures. These flags concern mass beyond the local projective chart, not failed numerical integration of the mapped Gaussian distribution.

## What remains approximate

A maximum inverse residual of 1.99e-9 certifies the declared moment fit, not recursive density closure. Across all 96 predeclared full-reference reports, average process-Bingham/reference density TV differences are 0.0227 and 0.0478 at kappa = 4 and 20. Average mean-operator differences are 0.0080 and 0.0160. The largest scalar-CDF discrepancies at the process-Bingham endpoints are 0.00507 and 0.01940. These density-projection errors are distinct from integration refinement errors.

Reference comparisons use the same sixteen trajectories on both sides. At kappa = 4, full-reference-minus-process terminal MSE is -0.000582 [-0.001475, 0.000312], and interval score is 0.000180 [-0.002816, 0.003175]. At kappa = 20 these differences are -0.002861 [-0.004214, -0.001508] and -0.009749 [-0.019911, 0.000413]. Do not compare the sixteen-trajectory reference mean directly with a 200-trajectory method mean.

Two of the 96 reference reports fail their predeclared acceptance checks. At kappa = 4, trial 0, step 16, interval-endpoint refinement is 0.02107 of the fine interval width, exceeding the 0.02 limit. At kappa = 20, trial 10, step 24, coarse degree-24 negative spectral mass is 1.00623e-9, just above the 1e-9 limit; the degree-32 prediction remains positive. `fixed_budget_spectral_diagnostic.json` diagnoses the latter at the unchanged budgets. Both failures remain in all tables. There is no success-selected reference subset, no unconditional reference-tail accuracy claim, and no numerical-superiority claim based on discarding these targets.

## Cost and scope

Under this concurrently loaded execution, mean prediction-plus-update time is about 5.0 ms per observation for process Bingham and 1.7–1.8 ms for the recursive Gaussian, including its short warm-up. This excludes predictive-score diagnostics. Median complete Bingham reports take about 0.28–0.41 s, versus 0.13–0.18 s for Gaussian reports. The same-parameter Laplace control shares the Bingham filtering cost; only its reporting calculation is cheaper. Warm-up timing is nested in prediction/update and must not be added again. These wall times are not hard real-time guarantees; the physical experiment supplies the relevant dedicated timing study.

The defensible finding is an empirical benefit from the global process update over this local recursive Gaussian in the matched control, especially with larger inter-observation motion. The same-parameter comparison does not establish a general premium for full-distribution reporting. The fixed-forgetting method is a diagnostic with beta = 0.85, not an independently tuned physical benchmark. None of these results establishes a calibrated robotic controller, superiority over position-based methods, or robustness to arbitrary model misspecification.
